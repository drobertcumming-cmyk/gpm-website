Gold IRA · How It Works

How it works

A Gold IRA lets you hold physical gold inside a retirement account that follows the same IRS rules as any other IRA. Same contribution limits, same tax treatment, same required minimum distributions. The only thing that changes is what you own and where it sits.

What a Gold IRA is

The structure was created by the Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997, which expanded Internal Revenue Code Section 408(m) to allow IRAs to hold specific forms of physical gold, silver, platinum, and palladium. Before that, IRAs could hold paper assets only.

Tax treatment follows the same rules as any other IRA. Traditional Gold IRA contributions are pre-tax and taxed on distribution. Roth Gold IRA contributions are post-tax and not taxed on qualified distribution. Required minimum distributions begin at age 73.

A Gold IRA has three roles, and they are separate.

The statute
IRC §408(m), expanded by the Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997.
Same rules as any IRA
Contribution limits, tax treatment, RMDs at 73.
The only difference
What the account holds. Physical bullion instead of paper.
1

Custodian

Administers your IRA. Independent of Grace. IRS-approved. You can call them directly to verify your account at any time.

2

Depository

Holds your physical metal. Independent of Grace. IRS-approved. Insured and audited.

3

Dealer

Supplies the metal and handles the paperwork that connects the three parties. This is where Grace sits.

How you fund it

You fund a Gold IRA by transferring or rolling over money from an existing retirement account. The four common sources are a 401(k) from a current or former employer, a traditional IRA at a bank or brokerage, a Roth IRA, or a SEP IRA — common for self-employed people.

The mechanics are the same in each case. The funds move directly from your existing account to your new IRA’s custodian. You never receive the money. There is no tax event. There is no penalty.

Two situations work slightly differently. An inherited IRA is subject to the SECURE Act’s distribution rules. A transfer from another Gold IRA company can move metal in kind, rather than converting to cash and back. Both are explained in their own pages: see Inherited IRA and Transfer Guide.

Four common origins

What you can hold

The IRS sets eligibility rules under Section 408(m). Standard IRS-eligible bullion is what Grace sells.

What we don’t sell

No numismatic, proof, or rare coins.

Most aren’t IRA-eligible, and the practice of selling them aggressively to IRA customers has been the subject of federal regulatory investigation.

Timeline

A typical Gold IRA setup takes between one and three weeks, end to end.

What controls the clock

Your existing custodian.

The time-limiting factor is almost always how quickly the prior custodian releases the funds. We have no control over that, and we say so when we set expectations.
