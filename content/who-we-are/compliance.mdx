# We published the standard. Hold us to it.

Grace Precious Metals operates under state dealer licensing where required, routes every Gold IRA holding through IRS-approved custodians and IRS-approved depositories, and maintains substantiation files on every comparative claim published on this website. The regulatory posture is not a footer line. It is the operational infrastructure of the business — and it is on this page before you speak to anyone.

## How a Gold IRA is structured

A Gold IRA is a self-directed Individual Retirement Account that holds physical precious metals. Three parties are involved in every account.

The **dealer** — that is us — sells you the metals and buys them back when you sell. Our role is the transaction, the pricing, and the counsel that surrounds the decision.

The **custodian** — an IRS-approved trust company — administers the retirement account itself. The custodian holds the account in your name, handles the tax reporting, and processes the rollover or transfer paperwork from your existing retirement plan. The custodian is a separate legal entity from Grace.

The **depository** — an IRS-approved, insured storage facility — physically holds the metals on your behalf. The depository is also a separate legal entity from Grace. Your metals are held in your name through your custodian; they are not held on Grace's balance sheet.

This three-party structure is the IRS-mandated framework for Gold IRAs. It exists to protect you. Your metals sit with an insured third-party depository, administered by an independent custodian, whose relationship to you does not depend on Grace's continued existence.

## Regulatory framework

The precious metals dealer category operates under several overlapping regulatory regimes.

The **Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)** has jurisdiction over precious metals fraud and has brought enforcement actions against Gold IRA dealers for misrepresentation of pricing, coin premiums, and buyback terms. Our published-pricing posture, bullion-only catalogue, and buyback-at-spot commitment are structural responses to the pattern of harm documented in those enforcement records.

The **Federal Trade Commission (FTC)** applies substantiation standards to advertising and endorsement claims. Every forward-looking or comparative claim we publish is supported by a substantiation file. When we work with ambassadors or creators, material-connection disclosure is required under FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) and we enforce that requirement on every piece of sponsored content.

The **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** does not regulate physical precious metals directly, but it does regulate investment advice, and we are careful not to provide any. Grace is a dealer, not an advisor.

**State regulators** — attorneys general, state dealer licensing boards, and securities regulators — apply consumer protection law to precious metals sales. We maintain compliance with applicable state frameworks.
