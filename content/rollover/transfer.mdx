# Already hold a Gold IRA? Here is the first question to ask.

If you currently hold a Gold IRA elsewhere and you are researching whether to transfer, there is one question that tells you almost everything you need to know before you move further. Ask your current provider, in writing, for a buyback quote on the amount you hold.

The buyback quote is what your current provider will pay you if you sell back to them today. It is the number you were not shown when you bought. It is also, when compared against the amount you paid on entry, the cleanest way to understand what the round-trip cost of your original purchase actually was.

This page walks through what the buyback quote tells you, how a custodian-to-custodian transfer works, what the mechanics cost, and how to decide whether a transfer is in your interest.

## Why the buyback quote is the load-bearing number

When most Gold IRA customers originally purchased, they were told a markup over spot. They were not necessarily told a buyback spread. Many did not know a buyback spread existed.

What typically happens on exit is this: the customer calls to sell back, and discovers that the buyback price is not the spot price — it is a marked-down figure below spot. The markdown is the second margin. In combination with the entry markup, it produces the real round-trip cost of the original decision.

Getting the buyback quote, in writing, from your current provider is how you see that number. Not every provider will put it in writing. Some will delay. Some will route you to a retention specialist. Pay attention to those responses — they are information too.

If the buyback quote, compared to the amount you paid on entry and the current spot price of the metals, reveals that your round-trip cost is materially higher than you had understood, you have the information you need to decide whether to transfer. Whether to act on that information is your decision. What we can tell you is how a transfer works if you decide to make one.

## How a custodian-to-custodian transfer works

A transfer between Gold IRA custodians is a trustee-to-trustee transfer: funds move directly from your current custodian to your new custodian without being distributed to you personally. There is no taxable event. There is no 20% withholding. There is no one-rollover-per-year restriction.

The mechanics are straightforward. You open an account with a new IRS-approved custodian (Grace's advisor walks you through this). Your new custodian requests the transfer from your current custodian. Your current custodian sells the metals — this is where the buyback quote becomes the actual number — and transfers the cash proceeds to the new custodian. The new custodian then uses the transferred funds to purchase metals at Grace at the published 11.1% spread. The metals are shipped to your IRS-approved depository and held in your name.

A transfer typically completes within ten to fifteen business days. The timing depends on how quickly your current custodian releases funds, which varies.

## What a transfer costs

Grace's entry cost is the 11.1% spread on the transferred amount. There is no admin fee. There is no setup fee for the transfer itself — your new custodian may charge an account-opening fee, which is disclosed on their fee schedule and is separate from Grace's pricing.

Your current provider may apply an account-closing fee, a final-year storage fee, or other fees on exit. Those are between you and them; we do not see them and we do not receive them. When you request the buyback quote, also request the full exit fee schedule. It should not be a surprise at the point of transfer.

## Should you transfer?

This is your decision. What we can tell you is the framework.

A transfer makes sense when the forward-going cost of staying — the buyback spread you would absorb on eventual exit, plus any ongoing provider fees — exceeds the entry cost of moving to a lower-priced provider plus the cost of the exit itself from your current provider. In most cases we have seen, once a customer has the buyback quote and the full exit fee schedule in hand, the calculation resolves clearly.

What we will not do is put pressure on the decision. Transfer when you are ready, from the information you have seen in writing. If the math tells you to stay, stay.

## Common questions

**Does the transfer produce a tax event?** A properly executed trustee-to-trustee transfer between IRS-approved custodians produces no taxable event.

**Do I have to sell the metals to transfer?** Generally yes, because metals held in a Gold IRA are held on your behalf by your current provider's custodian-and-depository structure. The metals are sold at the buyback price, the cash proceeds are transferred to the new custodian, and new metals are purchased at the new dealer. In-kind transfers of physical metal between custodians are possible in some cases and should be discussed with both custodians if you want to explore that path.

**What if my current provider refuses to give me a buyback quote in writing?** That refusal is information. A provider who will not put the exit number in writing is a provider who is aware that the number, in writing, is going to be uncomfortable. Your advisor at Grace can walk you through how to escalate the request through your current custodian if the dealer is non-responsive.

**How long does a transfer take?** Typically ten to fifteen business days. Timing varies based on how quickly your current provider releases funds.

**Will I pay the 11.1% twice — once on my original purchase and again on the transfer?** You paid your original entry cost when you originally purchased. On transfer, you pay Grace's 11.1% spread on the transferred amount. Whether the transfer is in your interest depends on whether the go-forward savings — on buyback, on ongoing fees, on any future purchases — exceed the new entry cost.
