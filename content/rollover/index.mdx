# The Gold IRA does not have to be complicated.

A rollover from a conventional retirement account into a Gold IRA is a well-established IRS-sanctioned process. Three parties, four account types, ten to fifteen business days. This page walks through the mechanics, the timing, the tax treatment, and the costs — in plain English, without the jargon the category uses to keep the decision out of reach.

Four account types are covered: 401(k), traditional IRA, Roth IRA, and SEP IRA. The inherited IRA case is a separate page because the SECURE Act rules make it materially different. The transfer case — if you already hold a Gold IRA elsewhere and are researching whether to move — is also a separate page.

## What a Gold IRA rollover actually is

A Gold IRA is a self-directed Individual Retirement Account that holds physical precious metals instead of paper assets. A rollover is the movement of funds from your existing retirement account into a new self-directed IRA at an IRS-approved custodian, where the funds are then used to purchase metals that are stored in an IRS-approved depository on your behalf.

Three entities are involved. The dealer — that is us — handles the metal. The custodian — an IRS-approved trust company — administers the retirement account. The depository — an IRS-approved insured storage facility — physically holds the metals. The three parties are separate, and the structure is the structure the IRS requires for this asset class.

## Which accounts can be rolled over

**401(k).** Funds in a current-employer 401(k) generally cannot be rolled over while you are still employed by the sponsoring employer, unless you are over 59½ and the plan permits an in-service rollover. Funds in a former-employer 401(k) can generally be rolled over freely. The same logic applies to 403(b) and similar employer-sponsored retirement plans.

**Traditional IRA.** Funds in a traditional IRA can be rolled over or transferred into a self-directed Gold IRA at any time, subject to the annual one-rollover-per-year rule that governs 60-day indirect rollovers. A trustee-to-trustee transfer (see below) is not subject to that rule.

**Roth IRA.** Funds in a Roth IRA can be rolled over or transferred into a self-directed Roth Gold IRA. The Roth structure is preserved — contributions have already been taxed, qualified distributions remain tax-free, and the rollover itself produces no additional taxable event.

**SEP IRA.** SEP IRA funds can be rolled over into a self-directed Gold IRA following the same mechanics as traditional IRA rollovers.

## Trustee-to-trustee transfer vs. 60-day rollover

There are two mechanisms for moving funds, and the distinction matters.

A **trustee-to-trustee transfer** is a direct movement of funds from your current custodian or plan administrator to your new self-directed IRA custodian. You do not receive the funds personally. There is no 20% mandatory tax withholding. There is no one-rollover-per-year limit. Most rollovers into a Gold IRA are executed as trustee-to-trustee transfers because the mechanics are cleaner.

A **60-day rollover** is the alternative mechanism, in which the funds are distributed to you personally and you have sixty days to deposit them into the new custodian. This mechanism carries two material risks: if the sixty-day window is missed, the distribution becomes a taxable event and, if you are under 59½, a 10% early-withdrawal penalty may apply; and for 401(k) distributions, a 20% mandatory federal tax withholding typically applies at the point of distribution, which you then have to make up from personal funds to complete the rollover of the full amount.

For most customers, the trustee-to-trustee transfer is the correct choice. Your Grace advisor will walk you through the mechanics with your current plan administrator and your new custodian.

## The timing

A typical rollover from initiation to completion runs between ten and fifteen business days. Faster than that is possible in some cases; slower than that is possible when the current plan administrator is slow to release funds, which happens. The sequence is:

**Days 1–2.** Account opened with your IRS-approved custodian.

**Days 3–7.** Rollover or transfer paperwork submitted to your current plan administrator.

**Days 7–12.** Funds released by the current plan and received by your new custodian.

**Days 12–15.** Metals purchased at the published 11.1% spread and shipped by Grace to your IRS-approved depository, held in your name.

Your advisor tracks the timeline and keeps you informed at each step.

## The tax treatment

A properly executed trustee-to-trustee transfer or direct rollover is a non-taxable event. Funds move from one qualified retirement account to another qualified retirement account, the tax-deferred status is preserved, and no distribution is recognized by the IRS.

Your IRS-approved custodian issues the tax reporting. Grace is not your tax advisor and does not file your taxes. Consult a qualified tax professional with questions about how a rollover interacts with your specific circumstances.

## Common questions

**Do I have to roll over my entire retirement account?** No. Most customers roll over a portion. The appropriate allocation depends on your circumstances and should be discussed with qualified professionals.

**Can I take physical possession of the metals?** Not while the metals are held in the Gold IRA. IRS rules require IRA-held metals to be stored at an IRS-approved depository. When you take a distribution from the IRA, you can receive metals (in-kind distribution) or cash (we buy them back at spot), subject to applicable tax treatment at the point of distribution.

**What happens if I change my mind mid-process?** If the rollover has not yet been executed, nothing is lost — the account can be closed with your custodian. If the rollover is complete and metals have been purchased, you can sell them back to us at spot on the day, and your custodian can move the resulting cash back into a conventional IRA.

**Does Grace handle the paperwork?** Grace's advisors walk you through the paperwork in coordination with your custodian. The custodian is the legal administrator of the IRA; certain forms can only be submitted by you or by the custodian.
