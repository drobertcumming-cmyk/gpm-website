# The people who come after you should not be left to negotiate with anyone.

A Gold IRA is not only a decision for today. It is an account your family will eventually inherit — and under SECURE Act rules, most non-spouse beneficiaries will have to handle it on a ten-year clock. Grace publishes the buyback policy. We sell standard bullion only. Our pricing is on the page. The mechanics should be clean when the people who come after you have to handle them.

The SECURE Act of 2019 materially changed the rules that govern inherited IRAs for most non-spouse beneficiaries. If you have inherited an IRA since 2020, or are planning for the inheritance of an IRA, the old rules you may remember — stretch-IRA distributions over the beneficiary's lifetime — no longer apply in most cases. This page explains the new rules and how a Gold IRA fits within them.

## What changed

Before the SECURE Act, non-spouse beneficiaries of a traditional or Roth IRA could generally stretch distributions over their own life expectancy — a "stretch IRA." Under the SECURE Act, for most non-spouse beneficiaries who inherited an IRA on or after January 1, 2020, the stretch is replaced with a **ten-year rule**: the inherited IRA must be fully distributed by the end of the tenth calendar year after the original account owner's death.

There are exceptions. "Eligible designated beneficiaries" — surviving spouses, minor children of the account owner, disabled or chronically ill beneficiaries, and beneficiaries not more than ten years younger than the decedent — can still generally use life-expectancy-based distributions. Most adult children and grandchildren who inherit IRAs do not qualify for these exceptions and are subject to the ten-year rule.

IRS guidance on how the ten-year rule interacts with required minimum distributions during the ten-year window has continued to evolve since the SECURE Act was passed. Under Treasury regulations finalised in mid-2024, certain beneficiaries are required to take annual RMDs during the ten-year period in addition to the full distribution by year ten, depending on the age of the decedent at death. The specific RMD treatment depends on individual circumstances and should be reviewed with a qualified tax professional. Our description of the rule on this page reflects final regulations as of the date shown in the disclaimer at the foot of this page; Treasury guidance continues to be refined, and the treatment applicable to your circumstances should be verified with a qualified tax professional before you act on it.

## Spousal beneficiaries

A surviving spouse has the broadest set of options. The spouse can treat the inherited IRA as their own, roll it into their own existing IRA, or leave it as an inherited IRA subject to beneficiary rules. Each option produces different tax and distribution consequences. Spousal beneficiaries should consult a qualified tax professional before making the election, because the election materially affects the distribution schedule and the surviving spouse's own retirement planning.

## Non-spouse beneficiaries under the ten-year rule

For most non-spouse beneficiaries, the inherited IRA must be fully distributed by the end of the tenth calendar year after the account owner's death. Distributions are taxable as ordinary income in the year they are taken (for traditional IRAs; Roth inherited IRAs follow Roth rules). The timing of distributions across the ten-year window affects the total tax paid on the inheritance, because the beneficiary's marginal tax rate may vary year to year. The specifics of how to schedule those distributions are a planning question that should be taken to a qualified tax professional.

## How a Gold IRA fits

An inherited IRA can generally be rolled into a self-directed Gold IRA under the same mechanics as a standard IRA rollover, subject to the beneficiary's distribution obligations. The ten-year rule applies to the inherited IRA regardless of what assets it holds — whether paper or precious metals.

For a beneficiary planning to distribute the inheritance across the ten-year window, a Gold IRA structure can provide diversification within the inheritance during the holding period. ## What to do next

If you have inherited an IRA or are planning for inheritance and are considering a Gold IRA component:

First, confirm your beneficiary category (spouse, eligible designated beneficiary, or other non-spouse beneficiary) with your tax professional, because the category determines the distribution rules.

Second, confirm the account owner's date of death and the associated distribution-window dates, because those dates are load-bearing for the ten-year rule.
