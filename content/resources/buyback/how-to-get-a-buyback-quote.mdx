# How to get a buyback quote from your current Gold IRA provider.

If you currently hold a Gold IRA and you want to understand what you actually paid on entry — or what you would receive if you sold today — the buyback quote is the load-bearing piece of information. This article walks through how to request it, what to ask for, and how to interpret the answer.

## Why you want this in writing

A buyback quote given verbally on a phone call is not the same as a buyback quote given in writing. Verbal quotes drift, are not time-stamped, are not preserved for your records, and are difficult to compare against the provider's documented policy. Written quotes are auditable.

If the buyback quote turns out to reveal a materially different round-trip cost than you had understood, a written record is the starting point of any conversation with the provider, with a financial advisor, or with a regulatory body if the case rises to that level.

Written means email at minimum. Formal written policy documentation is stronger. A letter or PDF with the provider's letterhead is strongest.

## What to ask for, specifically

Here is the request framework. You can adapt the language; the elements matter more than the exact phrasing.

**Element 1 — the buyback price.** "Please provide a current buyback quote on my holding of [ounces] of [product], expressed as the price per ounce you would pay me if I sold today."

**Element 2 — the spot reference.** "Please specify the spot gold price you are using as the reference for the quote, and the source of that spot price."

**Element 3 — the effective spread.** "Please express the effective buyback spread as a percentage — the gap between the spot price and your buyback price, divided by the spot price."

**Element 4 — contractual status.** "Please confirm whether the buyback quote is contractually guaranteed, or whether it is indicative and subject to change at the point of exit."

**Element 5 — product variation.** "Please confirm whether the buyback spread varies across products in my account, and if so, please itemize the buyback spread applied to each product type I hold."

**Element 6 — timing.** "Please confirm how long this quote is valid, and the next date on which it would be re-quoted."

Send the request by email. Keep a copy.

## How to read the response

**A complete, prompt response.** The provider comes back within a few business days with all six elements addressed, in writing. This is what you want, and it is the information you need to evaluate the round-trip cost of your holding.

**A partial response.** The provider addresses some elements but not others — typically, they will provide a buyback price but will not specify the contractual status or the product-by-product variation. Follow up. Ask for the missing elements explicitly.

**A response that routes you to a phone call.** "Please call our retention specialist to discuss your buyback." A phone-call routing on a written request is itself information. Your reply: "I would prefer to have this information in writing before any call. Please provide the written quote first."

**A delayed response.** More than two weeks is a delay worth noting. Follow up. If the delay persists, the custodian administering your IRA may be able to assist — the custodian has a fiduciary obligation to the account and can in some cases compel a response from the dealer.

**A refusal.** A provider that will not put the buyback quote in writing is a provider you should be careful with. Document the refusal. Consider escalating through your custodian, and consider whether the refusal itself is sufficient reason to initiate a transfer.

## What to do with the information

Once you have the written quote, calculate your round-trip cost.

Take the amount you originally paid. Take the amount you would receive today if you sold at the buyback quote. The difference — adjusted for any change in spot price since you bought — is the total dealer revenue on your holding across the round trip. Expressed as a percentage of what you originally paid, this is your round-trip cost.

If the round-trip cost matches what you had understood at the point of purchase, the provider has delivered on the pricing they quoted. If the round-trip cost is materially higher than what you had understood, you now have written evidence of the gap, and a basis for deciding whether to transfer.
