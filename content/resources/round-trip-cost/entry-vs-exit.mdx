# Entry cost vs. exit cost: why they are not the same.

The two numbers live on opposite sides of the Gold IRA transaction, and the industry treats them asymmetrically.

The entry cost is what you pay to buy gold on the day of the purchase. It is the markup over spot, sometimes combined with admin or setup fees, and it is visible at the point of the transaction — it has to be, because it is what appears on your invoice.

The exit cost is the gap between the spot price on the day you sell and the buyback price the dealer actually pays you. It is not visible at the point of purchase. It is typically not discussed in marketing materials. It is sometimes only quoted on the day of exit, years after the original decision was made.

This asymmetry is not accidental. It is how the category has evolved. And it produces a specific consequence: customers evaluate providers on the visible number (entry cost) rather than the less-visible number (exit cost), even though the exit cost is often the more consequential one across the life of the holding.

## The exit cost problem

Three things make exit cost particularly hard to evaluate at the point of purchase.

**It is not shown.** Most Gold IRA marketing states the entry markup. It does not state the buyback spread. A customer evaluating providers has the entry cost on the page and has to work to find the exit cost.

**It is not stable.** Buyback spreads can vary across providers, across products within the same provider's catalogue, and across market conditions. A customer who was told verbally that the buyback would be "at or near spot" at the point of purchase may discover at the point of exit that the actual buyback is meaningfully below spot.

**It is not always contractually committed.** Some providers treat buyback as an indicative quote — a number that applies at the point of exit, subject to market conditions at that time. Without a contractual commitment, the exit cost is whatever the provider decides it is on the day.

## Why exit cost often exceeds entry cost in financial consequence

Two reasons.

**Entry cost is a one-time percentage of a known number.** You pay 11.1% (or 8%, or 15%, or whatever the entry markup is) of the amount you roll over, once, at the beginning. The dollar impact is known and bounded.

**Exit cost is a percentage of a number that has grown with the gold price.** If gold has appreciated during your hold period — which is typically why you held it — the exit cost is a percentage of a larger dollar amount than the entry cost was calculated against. A 10% exit spread on a $150,000 purchase that has grown to $200,000 over the hold period is $20,000. A 10% entry markup on the original $150,000 was $15,000. The exit cost, even at the same percentage spread, is the larger dollar number.

## What this means for your evaluation

When you compare Gold IRA providers, ask for both numbers.

The entry cost — the total percentage above spot you will pay on the day of purchase, inclusive of any admin, setup, or product-premium fees.

The exit cost — the buyback spread, in writing, with a contractual commitment to the spread structure over the life of the holding.

Evaluate on the combination. Not on either alone.
