# Round-trip cost: the one number that tells you what a Gold IRA actually costs.

Most Gold IRA marketing focuses on a single number: the entry markup. Sometimes called the spread, sometimes called the premium over spot, sometimes absorbed into the quoted per-coin price. This number answers the question: "what am I paying the dealer to buy the gold?" It is not, however, the number that tells you what a Gold IRA decision actually costs across the life of the holding.

The number that tells you that is round-trip cost.

## Definition

Round-trip cost is the total cost to you, expressed as a percentage of your original purchase amount, across the full cycle of owning and eventually selling a Gold IRA holding. It has three components.

**Entry cost.** The markup over spot you paid on the day of purchase. Expressed as a percentage of the amount you rolled over. This includes any admin fee, setup fee, or product premium absorbed into the per-coin price.

**Exit cost.** The gap between the spot price on the day you sell and the buyback price your dealer actually pays you. If the buyback is at spot, the exit cost is zero. If the buyback is 10% below spot, the exit cost is 10% of your holding at the time of exit.

**Ongoing cost.** The custodian and depository fees you pay during the holding period. These are separate from the dealer's pricing, typically small as an annual percentage (custodian fees in the $80–$225 range; depository fees in the 0.5%–1.0% range), but they compound over the hold period.

Round-trip cost is the sum of all three, expressed as a percentage of the original purchase amount.

## Why this matters

The marketing asymmetry in the category is that entry cost is highly visible and exit cost is not. A customer who compares dealers on entry cost alone may choose the dealer with the lowest entry cost and still pay a much higher round-trip cost than a customer who chose a dealer with a higher entry cost but a narrower or zero buyback spread.

A worked example, described qualitatively.

Consider two hypothetical dealers.

**The first dealer** quotes a lower entry markup than Grace, but applies a buyback spread — meaning when the customer eventually sells, the dealer pays less than the market spot price for the metals. On the day of purchase, this dealer looks cheaper. Across the full cycle of ownership, the dealer earns twice: once on the entry markup, once on the buyback spread. The second earning is not disclosed at the point of purchase.

**The second dealer** quotes a higher entry markup but commits to buying back at spot, with no spread on exit. On the day of purchase, this dealer looks more expensive. Across the full cycle of ownership, the dealer earns once, on the entry markup. The exit produces no additional dealer revenue.

A customer comparing these two dealers on entry markup alone would choose the first dealer. A customer comparing on round-trip cost — the combination of entry markup, ongoing fees, and exit spread across the full hold period — may reach a different conclusion, depending on the specific numbers each dealer discloses in writing. The principle to take away is that the entry number, viewed in isolation, cannot tell you which dealer is more economical over the life of the holding. Only round-trip cost can.

## The category-level numbers

Published research on Gold IRA industry pricing practices, combined with documented enforcement records, places the industry-typical round-trip cost on a standard Gold IRA rollover in a range between roughly 17% and 33%. The lower end reflects lower-markup dealers operating with modest buyback spreads; the upper end reflects dealers whose product mix includes significant numismatic or premium coin sales, whose entry markups are materially higher than bullion markups, and whose buyback spreads on those products are correspondingly wider.

Grace's round-trip cost on a standard bullion rollover is the 11.1% entry spread plus the ongoing custodian and depository fees. There is no exit cost from Grace.

## How to calculate your own round-trip cost

If you currently hold a Gold IRA, you can calculate your round-trip cost directly.

**Step 1 — your entry cost.** Find your original purchase invoice. The entry cost is the difference between what you paid and the spot price of the metals on the day of purchase, expressed as a percentage of what you paid. If your invoice does not clearly separate spot price from markup, your custodian or dealer should be able to provide the breakdown on request.

**Step 2 — your exit cost.** Request a written buyback quote from your current dealer (the mechanics are on our guide). Compare the buyback price per ounce to the current spot price. The gap, as a percentage of spot, is your exit cost.

**Step 3 — your ongoing cost.** Look at your custodian and depository fee schedules. Calculate the annual fee as a percentage of your holding value. Multiply by the number of years you expect to hold.

**Step 4 — total.** Add the three. That is your round-trip cost.

## What Grace's round-trip cost looks like

On Grace's standard $150,000 rollover:

Entry cost: 11.1% ($16,650).

Exit cost: 0% (buyback at spot).

Ongoing cost, assuming a five-year hold at category-typical custodian and depository fees: approximately 4–5% of the original amount cumulatively ($6,000–$7,500).

Total round-trip cost: approximately 15–16% of the original rollover, over a five-year hold.

On an industry-typical 17%–33% round-trip cost, the same holding produces a total cost of $25,500 to $49,500 over the same period. The difference is substantial, and it is the gap Grace is built to close.

## The summary

Round-trip cost is the only number that captures what a Gold IRA holding actually costs. Entry cost alone is incomplete — exit cost and ongoing costs matter at least as much, and in many cases more. When you evaluate any Gold IRA provider, ask for the round-trip cost on your specific holding, in writing. If the provider will only discuss entry cost, that is information.
